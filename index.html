A wizard battle for the ZX Spectrum Next. Do not meddle.

Summon. Bluff. Be the last wizard standing. Up to eight wizards meet in an arena with a book of spells each. Call up dragons and goblins, plant enchanted woods, raise the dead, and cast creatures that may not be real at all. Every other wizard wants you gone.

Features

A battle of wits, wands and wild creatures

Two to eight wizards

Play against friends round one Next, against the computer, or both. Pick each wizard's name, look and robe colour. A look-away screen keeps each player's spell book secret.

Teams and handicaps

Put wizards on teams, so a parent and child can take on the computer together. Give younger players stars for better odds and a bigger spell book.

A trophy cabinet

Twelve trophies to win. Kit out a wizard in full and they become a golden champion. Raise a dragon, fell a giant with a goblin, win by pure illusion, or cast a dragon against the odds. Every trophy is kept on the SD card, with the name of the first wizard to win it.

Spells you can see

Every spell streaks across the arena to its target, as in 1985: lightning crackles, bolts blaze, ice shards fly and arrows whistle.

Pick your arena

Start on an empty field, among ruined walls, in a grove of magic trees anyone can climb into, or around a fortress waiting to be claimed. Fight on plain black, as in 1985, among stars drifting past at two depths, on flagstones, sand, grass, ice or a giant chessboard.

Your battle, your rules

Choose how many rounds: when the last one ends, the wizard with the most points wins. Speed up the computer wizards' turns. Leave a long battle with Save and quit, and carry on another day.

Classic or Next

Play with the full set of spells and creatures, or switch to Classic for just the 1985 originals.

Know your odds

Point at an enemy before you strike and the game tells you your chance to win.

A new spell book every game

Over fifty spells, dealt at random. Make the best of what you get: no two battles play the same.

Is it real?

Any creature can be cast as an illusion. Illusions always work, but one Disbelieve makes them vanish. Bluff, or call the bluff.

Law and Chaos

Every spell that works tips the world towards Law or Chaos, and a meter above the arena shows the tug-of-war. Spells on the winning side get easier; the other side's get harder.

A whole bestiary

Goblins, elves, bears, ogres, eagles and a red dragon. The undead can only be hurt by magic or by other undead.

New creatures too

A troll that regrows, a phoenix reborn from fire, a banshee whose scream freezes, a salamander that leaves flames, a walking tree and more.

Ride into battle

Summon a horse, unicorn, pegasus or gryphon and climb on. Shelter in a magic castle until it crumbles.

Shape the arena

Plant enchanted woods, build walls, sink the ground into swamp, and loose gooey blobs and magic fire that spread every round. Or shake it all down with an earthquake.

Raise the dead

Fallen creatures leave their bodies where they fall. The right spell brings them back, on your side.

Arm your wizard

Magic swords, armour, wings, a bow, and Shadow Form. Teleport out of trouble, turn an enemy into a frog, or steal a spell from their book.

Made for the Next

Full-colour pictures on the Next's high-resolution screen, written from scratch in Z80 assembly.

Secrets to find

A few names unlock something special. And sit in an enchanted tree long enough and it may teach you a spell nobody else can get.

How to play

Choose, cast, move

The keys

Q A O PMove the cursor up, down, left and right, or pick from a list. A joystick works too, even for typing names: up and down turn the letter, fire takes it.
SPACEChoose: cast the spell, pick the piece or square
0 / ENTERDone: skip a spell, stop moving, end your turn
IRead about a spell in your book
O / POn a wizard's card or the battle options: change the setting
Y / NAnswer a question, like casting a creature as an illusion
NWhen moving: jump to your next piece that hasn't moved
HHelp, any time: how to play, every key, and tips
MMusic off or on, any time
SThe wizards' voices off or on, any time

The goal

Be the last wizard alive. When a wizard falls, everything they summoned goes with them.

Each round

Every wizard chooses a spell in secret, or looks over the board first, as in 1985. Then the spells are cast, one wizard at a time. Then each wizard moves their creatures and themselves, and fights.

Fighting

Move into an enemy to attack it: your combat against its defence, plus a roll each. Pick a square further off and a walker finds its own way there. Some creatures fly over the others, and some shoot from afar. Next to an enemy, you may be stuck fighting it.

Before you go

Choose New battle. The setup list starts ready: SPACE begins a battle between you and one computer wizard. To change things, move to Battle options (how many wizards, Classic or Next rules, the arena floor, free Disbelieve) or to a wizard, and press SPACE to open their card: name, look, robe colour, human or computer (easy, normal or hard), team and head start. O/P change each setting, and the line at the bottom says what it does. After a battle, SPACE plays again with the same wizards and 0 goes back to the menu.

Tips

From wizards who went before

  • A dragon is hard to cast. Cast it as an illusion and it always works... until someone disbelieves it.
  • Disbelieve is never used up. If an enemy creature looks too good to be true, try it.
  • Keep your wizard out of reach. Creatures can be replaced; you can't.
  • A castle keeps you safe while it stands. It won't stand forever.
  • Watch the world's balance at the top of the spell book: tip it your way before casting the big spells.
  • Undead shrug off ordinary blows. Bring a magic weapon, or undead of your own.

Download

Play it now

It's free. One file, for a ZX Spectrum Next or an emulator.

Latest build: v1.0 (build 171), 30 October 2026, 544 KB. What's new in it

Download the .nex
  1. On a real Next: copy affairs-of-wizards.nex to your SD card. Pick it in the Browser and press ENTER.
  2. In an emulator: open the .nex file in CSpect. In CSpect, Escape closes the emulator: start it with -esc to stop that.
  3. Players: gather round one keyboard. Humans take turns; the computer plays the rest.
